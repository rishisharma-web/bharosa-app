If a proposal fails the check

Bad JSON, an unknown customer, a ₹ figure not in our data, or an 8-second timeout → the rule or template result is used instead, and logged as such.

Never AI

Score · limits · ledger · payments · the 20% split · Stock Advance sizing · mandates · fraud checks.

AI (20 s). "AI handles what's messy: Kannada and Hindi speech, conversations, explanations. Code handles money: scores, limits, payments. The AI suggests; it never decides."
Why this split: LLMs are good at messy language, many languages and code-mixing, polite writing and explanations. They are bad at exact arithmetic, consistency, auditability and not inventing facts. So we use them only where their strengths matter.
What "agent" means here

An agent = LLM + one job + a validator + a fallback.

one joboptional read-only toolsstrict JSON outputvalidatorfallback Rules always run first; the LLM is only called when they can't answer.

Agents read, never write

Tools only fetch. Any change goes through deterministic services, usually after a human taps.

Every result is labelled

The activity log shows rule, ai or template for each decision.

AI off? Still works

With the switch off, every flow runs on rules and templates. A test makes sure no AI call is made.

"In Bharosa an agent is a narrow thing: an LLM with one job, a strict JSON format, a validator and a fallback. Simple inputs like 'Ramesh 340' never touch the LLM. The LLM only proposes; the validator decides; if anything goes wrong, the rule or template answer is used and the product keeps working."
If asked: the demo runs with AI off by default (SARVAM_ENABLED=false); test_ai_off.py asserts zero outbound calls.
The agents, and why each one

Four agents, each where rules alone fall short.

Speech

Job: voice → text; one short Kannada line back

Why AI: speech recognition is inherently AI

Never: decides anything

Counter Agent

Job: "Ramesh ne 340 ka saaman liya" → {customer, amount}

Why AI: many languages, code-mixing, number words

Never: invents a customer or amount; sets a limit

Recovery Agent

Job: polite reminders; "salary 1 tareekh ko" → promise ₹ on 1 Nov

Why AI: replies are open-ended; templates can't read them

Never: changes dues; accepts a promise outside policy

Growth Agent (Munim)

Job: words the weekly plan; answers "How do I pay Sharma on Friday?"

Why AI: turns several data sources into plain advice

Never: changes a number or the ranking; acts without a tap

Checks: spoken line keeps the same ₹ figures

Checks: customer in the list · amount ₹1–₹50,000

Checks: only ₹ = dues · promise ≤ dues, within 30 days

Checks: same actions, code's order · every ₹ from the facts

Why not more agents?

Each one adds non-determinism, latency and cost. Scores, limits, payments, the split, advance sizing, auto-pay and fraud checks are exact maths: they stay code.

Why not zero agents?

Then the shopkeeper types exact formats at a busy counter, reminders are generic and replies are ignored. That's just another khata app.

"We didn't add agents for the sake of it. There are exactly four, and each one sits where the input is genuinely messy human language. Look at the bottom of every card: what it can never do. And the amber row: the check its output must pass."
Judge question "why agents at all?": only where the input is messy human language; each output is validated by code, with a fallback.

Containing non-determinism

Six controls keep the AI honest.

1Strict output formats

The LLM must return JSON of a fixed shape. Anything else is rejected.

2Grounding checks

Every ₹ figure and every customer the AI names must already exist in our data.

3Rules first, AI second

"Ramesh 340" never reaches the LLM. Faster, cheaper, predictable.

4Humans approve money

The merchant taps Approve or Accept; the customer confirms every credit.

5Fallbacks everywhere

AI down, slow or wrong? Templates and rules take over. The product keeps working.

6In production, measure

Phrase test sets per language, accuracy monitoring, a log of every proposal and verdict.

"We use AI where the world is messy (speech, languages, conversations, explanations) and code where money moves. The AI can suggest; it can never decide or pay."
This is the one-sentence answer for judges; say the quote verbatim. If asked about hallucinated numbers: control 2. If asked about the AI being down on stage: control 5, and the demo already runs with AI off.

Risks & answers · 1 of 2

The questions we expect, and how the design answers them.

1
Isn't this just Khatabook?
Khatabook records. We check risk before credit, get the customer's confirmation, win new customers through the network, collect automatically and fund stock.
Built in: Guardrail · Scan-to-Confirm · Portable Trust · Stock Advance
2
Why would a customer confirm their own debt?
One scan, and on-time repayment of confirmed credit raises their score: higher limits at every Paytm shop.
Built in: score breakdown shown to the customer
3
Is AI deciding who gets credit?
No. A transparent formula decides; the shopkeeper has the final say. AI handles language only.
Built in: validator firewall · "Why?" reasons · override
4
Why agents at all?
Only where the input is messy human language. Each agent's output is validated by code, with a fallback if it fails.
Built in: rules first · AI switch · source on every log line
5
Who takes the loss if a customer defaults?
The shopkeeper, as today, but with far better information and a capped limit. Paytm takes no credit risk; advances come from regulated lenders, backed by verified receivables and repaid from 20% of collections.
Built in: overdue penalty · exposure cap · lender split
Answer in one breath each; point at the green "built in" line, which is what they can see in the demo. If a judge asks something here live, jump to this slide (press End, then ←).
Risks & answers · 2 of 2

Regulation, fraud, hardware and money.

6
Isn't sharing scores regulated?
Likely. So sharing is customer-controlled and revocable, disclosure is minimal, and production scoring would run through a licensed partner.
Built in: Share / Not now · Revoke · band + limit only
7
Can auto-pay debit on salary day?
Not instantly. UPI AutoPay needs notice the day before, so we collect on a date the customer picks in salary week, and they can skip.
Built in: cap · collection day · pre-debit notice
8
Can shops fake receivables to get advances?
We look for shared devices, instant confirmations, round amounts and money going in circles, and exclude suspicious entries from advance eligibility.
Built in: shared-device flag · verified-only eligibility
9
Does it need the new AI Soundbox?
No. Any Soundbox and the printed QR work; the AI Soundbox just adds voice and screens.
Built in: the QR request service works from any device
10
How does Paytm make money?
Lending distribution fees on advances and loans, bank fees for credit-line customers acquired at the counter, collections, and an optional premium tier, with no balance-sheet risk.
Built in: Stock Advance · credit line on UPI at the counter · AutoPay
Fraud-ring follow-up: "A dishonest shop could invent customers and confirm from one phone. A confirmation from a device already used by another customer at the same shop is flagged and kept out of advance eligibility."
